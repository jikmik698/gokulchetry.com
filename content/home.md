bio: Gokul Chetry paints not with brushes alone, but with the stubborn memory of monsoon clouds and the half-remembered hum of a hillside train. Working from a cluttered studio that smells of linseed oil and over-brewed tea, he layers his canvases with the bruised purples of dusk over the Brahmaputra and the startling ochre of a village wall at noon. Critics call his style "lyrical abstraction with a poet's conscience," though Chetry himself shrugs and says he is simply trying to catch the exact moment a shadow decides to leave a room. His figures—often faceless, elongated, caught mid-gesture—seem to be walking toward something they cannot name, which may be why collectors from Kochi to Copenhagen keep leaning closer, certain they've seen that same restless silhouette in their own dreams.
